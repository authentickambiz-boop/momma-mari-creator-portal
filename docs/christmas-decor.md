# Christmas decor brief

American Christmas decor, the kind on a house from the middle of the nineteenth century through the early twentieth, is a lit evergreen, greenery at the door and the mantel, and presents on the floor. The indoor tree came from Germany. Prince Albert’s tree was drawn in the *Illustrated London News* in 1848, and *Godey’s Lady’s Book* printed an American version in 1850. Families hung candles, glass baubles, tinsel, and homemade sweets, and later a string of electric bulbs. Edward H. Johnson wired eighty red, white, and blue bulbs around a parlor tree in New York in 1882. By the 1930s colored electric lights had replaced candles. Around that tree: a wreath, holly, a poinsettia, stockings, candy canes, and wrapped gifts.

## What belongs in the scene

- **Christmas tree.** A bright evergreen with a slightly uneven silhouette, a gold star, glass-ball ornaments in red, gold, and cream, a draped garland, and small glowing bulbs in the branches. Not a perfect triangle.
- **Wrapped gifts.** Boxes under the tree in red, green, and gold paper, with cream ribbons and bows. A candy cane leans on the pile.
- **Border lights.** A green wire along the top, bottom, and both sides of the viewport, with round bulbs in red, gold, cream, and green that glow and blink.
- **Falling snow.** Flakes cross the whole viewport. The painting also has snow in the air and on the ground.
- **Cottage and window glow.** A small house with snow on the roof and warm yellow windows, the way a lit parlor reads from the yard.
- **Wreath and stockings.** An evergreen wreath with red berries and a red bow, on the door. Two stockings, one red and one green, with cream cuffs, hung nearby.
- **Greenery.** Holly with red berries, a red poinsettia, and a snowy ground line. The portal adds a small snowman where the site leaves that gap open.

## What we leave out

No Santa, no named characters, no lyrics, and no photoreal snow. The center of the public homepage stays dark sky and glass so the headline can be read. The portal moves the tree to the other side.

## Motion

Snow falls and drifts, and stops when the visitor asks for reduced motion. Bulbs along the border and the bulbs in the tree ease between dim and bright, and they stop with the same setting. Phones keep the drawing, the snow, and the border lights, and drop the big lantern glows.
