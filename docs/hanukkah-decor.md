# Hanukkah decor brief

Hanukkah 2026 runs from sundown on Friday, December 4 through nightfall on Saturday, December 12. The scene stays a preview. It paints only with `?mm_preview=hanukkah`. The public calendar does not turn it on.

The picture is a blue, white, and silver night, drawn like cut paper: thick ink lines, slightly crooked houses, and a few warm gelt coins so the gold stays a small accent. Momma Mari’s public pages look in from the yard, at a row of little houses and a garland of Stars of David. The creator portal looks out from a desk: a frosted window on the left, dreidels on a blue cloth, and gelt in a dish. The phone drawing is the yard, with fewer pieces.

## The menorah

A hanukkiah in front of the painting is the part that changes.

- Before 5:00 p.m. Eastern on December 4, the branches are empty and a tag counts the days until Hanukkah. December 4 before that hour says the festival begins at sundown.
- Night 1 starts at 5:00 p.m. on December 4: the shamash, plus the candle on the viewer’s far right.
- Each next night lights at 5:00 p.m. Candles are added from right to left. Night 8, from 5:00 p.m. on December 11 through the end of December 12, is all eight candles and the shamash.
- Flames catch newest first. The shamash goes first, then the leftmost new candle, then the older ones toward the right.
- Flames flicker a little. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` leaves them still and lit.

`?mm_preview=hanukkah&mm_night=N` reviews one state. `N=0` is the countdown. `N=1` through `N=8` are those nights. Any other value is ignored.

## What else is in the picture

- **Stars of David** on a sagging string outdoors, and as a frost mark and a hanging mobile indoors.
- **Dreidels** in the snow on the public site, and on the table in the portal, with nun, gimel, hei, and shin scratched on.
- **Gelt**, a few foil coins, loose in the snow or piled in a dish.
- **Sparkles**, small and slow. They stop with reduced motion.

## What we leave out

No latkes as a mascot, no named characters, and no lyrics. The center of the page stays dark glass so the words can be read. The portal menorah sits on the other side from the public one.
