# Thanksgiving decor brief

American Thanksgiving decor is harvest abundance, not a costume pageant. Nineteenth-century tables used what the garden and orchard actually grew: pumpkins, corn, wheat, fruit, and fall foliage on the dining table or sideboard. Manufactured turkeys, paper pumpkins, and wheat bunches showed up in early twentieth-century guidebooks (Dennison, Beistle, and table books from about 1904–1924). The cornucopia, from Latin *cornu* + *copiae*, “horn of plenty,” is a much older emblem of a good harvest. It was not part of the 1621 meal. It became a Thanksgiving centerpiece because the day is about the year’s bounty. Style it by letting fruit and vegetables spill out of a woven horn.

## What belongs in the scene

- **Cornucopia.** A wicker horn, curl on the left and wide mouth to the right, overflowing the way a classic horn-of-plenty print does: a large pumpkin in the mouth, striped gourd, two ears of flint corn (often called Indian corn) with pale husks and mixed kernels, purple grapes and green grapes, red apples, a pale pumpkin, a sunflower, wheat sheaves, pinecones, acorns, berries, and maple leaves around the base.
- **Turkey.** A clear bird: fanned tail with feather eyes, body, wing, head, red snood, beak, and feet. Not a blob.
- **Harvest table.** Plaid runner, pumpkin pie and apple pie, candles. Plaid and candlelight are ordinary modern table dressing.
- **Field and porch.** Wheat sheaves, hay bales, a scarecrow in a plaid shirt, sunflowers, maple and oak leaves, an autumn wreath on a door or barn.

## What we leave out

No pilgrims, no Wampanoag or other Native figures, and no “first Thanksgiving” story scene. Flint corn is the vegetable, drawn as ears. The page center stays open enough that the headline can be read through the glass.

## Motion

Leaves drift and turn in the air. Wheat sways from the base, like a light wind, and stops when the visitor asks for reduced motion. Candle flames and the warm glow flicker. A border of leaves, gourds, and acorns frames the edges. Phones keep the drawing and the wheat, and drop the floating pieces.
